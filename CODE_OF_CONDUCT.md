# Code of Conduct

DisSysLab is a teaching project. Most of the people reading an
issue thread will be first-year undergraduates who are new to
distributed systems, new to open source, and reasonably worried
about looking foolish in public. The standard here follows from
that.

## What is expected

- **Assume the question is a good one.** If someone asks
  something the documentation already answers, the documentation
  probably did not answer it well enough. Point at the place and,
  where you can, improve it.
- **Be specific rather than dismissive.** "That won't work"
  helps nobody; "that won't work because `recv` blocks until a
  message arrives, so the loop never reaches the timer" teaches
  something.
- **Critique the code, not the person.** Say what the code does
  and what it should do instead.
- **Credit what you build on.** Cite the office, role, or paper
  your work came from.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments, whether about a
  person's identity, background, employer, institution, or
  ability.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any
  kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion, or repeatedly pushing past
  a "no".

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code
comments, and any space where someone is acting as a participant
in this project.

## Reporting

Report a concern to **K. Mani Chandy — kmchandy@gmail.com**.
Reports are read by the maintainer. Include what happened, where,
and roughly when; a link is usually enough. You will get an
acknowledgement, though not necessarily a fast one — see the note
on bandwidth in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).

If your report concerns the maintainer, and you are at Caltech,
you may instead use the Institute's own reporting channels.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit or remove contributions, ask someone to
stop, or block an account from the repository. Which of these
applies depends on the severity and on whether the behaviour
continues after it has been named. Where the situation allows it,
the first step is a private word rather than a public one.

## Scope

This document covers the DisSysLab repository and its issue and
pull-request threads. Conduct elsewhere is in scope only when it
gives a participant reason to fear for their safety in this
project's spaces.
