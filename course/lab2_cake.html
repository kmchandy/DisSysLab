DisSysLab · Lab 2

The cake room: a shared whiteboard

In Lab 1 every agent received only the messages its connections carried to it. This lab shows a different way for agents to work together: a shared whiteboard. Every agent reads everything every other agent wrote, and builds on it.

That makes a team able to solve problems that no member could solve alone. It also makes the team harder to predict: what each agent writes depends on what the others wrote first.

About 2 hoursNeeds: Lab 1 done, and your Anthropic keyCost: a few hundred model calls; start small

Step 1 · The case

Read this first: the puzzle, and who knows what

A cake vanished from the kitchen between 3 and 4 this afternoon. It was certainly one of four people: Ann, Ben, Cal or Dee, and nobody else.

Three detectives investigate. Each is an LLM agent, and each holds one piece of evidence that no other detective sees:

  • Pip has the kitchen door's key-card log. Dee's card was not used all day, and the kitchen has no other door.
  • Quinn has the receipts from the shops in town. Ben paid at the shoe shop at 3:30, and the shop's camera shows him there from 2 until 5.
  • Rae has the notes pinned to the fridge. One is a printout from the garden camera: Ann is in every frame from 2 until 5, weeding.

A fourth agent, Judy the judge, sees no evidence at all. She reads what the detectives wrote and gives the verdict.

Look at what each detective can work out alone. Each piece of evidence clears exactly one person, and leaves three:

No piece of evidence mentions Cal. Nobody can name Cal from their own evidence; the answer appears only when all three pieces are put together. That is what the whiteboard is for.

How the whiteboard works

The whiteboard does nothing on its own. It changes only when a detective writes on it, and every change goes out to the detectives. There are no rounds, and nobody waits for anybody.

  1. The start. Each detective is sent its own evidence and the board, still empty. It writes a note: what its evidence shows, and whom it now suspects: Ann, Ben, Cal, Dee, or unknown.
  2. Whenever the board changes. A note goes on the board the moment it arrives, and the new board is sent to every detective who has not seen it. A detective that is still thinking gets the newest board as soon as it finishes.
  3. Nothing new. A detective that reads the board and has nothing to add says so, and writes nothing. That is how the talking stops.
  4. The verdict. When no detective is thinking and every one has read every note the others wrote, nothing more will ever be written. Only then does Judy get the board. She names the person who took the cake, or says unknown.

Notice what nobody does. No detective sends anything to another detective, and nobody decides who should read what. Everything written goes to everyone. In Lab 1 you drew every connection yourself and knew exactly what each agent would receive. Here you know only that each agent will receive everything, and you cannot know in advance what that will be: the three detectives think at the same time, and whichever finishes first writes first. The detective who writes second has read the first note; the one who wrote first has not. Run the same office twice, on the same evidence, and the board can fill up in a different order, with different notes.

Before you run anything, write down your predictions.
  1. In its first note, whom will each detective name? Does it matter whether another note is already on the board when it writes?
  2. Will all three detectives end up naming Cal? How many notes will the board hold when the talking stops?
  3. If you ran the office ten times, would the board fill up in the same order? Would Judy give the same verdict every time?
You will check these in Steps 2 and 3. Keep them: a wrong prediction you can explain is worth more in your write-up than a right one you cannot.
