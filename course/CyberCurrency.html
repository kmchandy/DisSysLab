CyberCurrency

Goofycoin, Scroogecoin, and the double-spending problem

This lecture builds simple cryptocurrencies from the tools of the cryptography lecture: hash pointers, block chains and digital signatures. Agents are identified by their public keys. It follows section 1.5 of Narayanan, Bonneau, Felten, Miller and Goldfeder, Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies (Princeton University Press, 2016); see bitcoinbook.cs.princeton.edu.

Two simple cryptocurrencies

1. Goofycoin

Goofycoin has two rules.

  1. Goofy creates coins. He signs the statement “CreateCoin [uniqueCoinID]”. The new coin belongs to Goofy.
  2. An owner spends a coin by signing “Pay this coin to pk”, together with a hash pointer to the coin. The coin now belongs to the owner of pk. Anyone can check the chain of signatures back to Goofy.

Every signature in Goofycoin verifies, and nobody can forge one. Yet it has a fatal flaw.

The double-spending problem Signatures show who authorized a payment. They cannot show that the payer had not already spent the coin. For that, everyone must agree on one history of all transactions.

2. Scroogecoin

Scroogecoin fixes double spending with a trusted party, Scrooge, who keeps the one history. Scrooge publishes an append-only ledger: a block chain of transactions, with the hash pointer to its head signed by Scrooge. Agents are identified by their public keys.

  • CreateCoins creates new coins. Each coin has a value and an owner’s public key. A coin is named by (transaction id, index): coin (1, 0) is the first coin created by transaction 1.
  • PayCoins consumes some coins and creates new coins of the same total value, for the payees. It must be signed by the owner of every coin it consumes.

Scrooge appends a PayCoins transaction only if it is valid:

  1. the consumed coins exist and have not already been consumed;
  2. the total value consumed equals the total value created;
  3. it is signed by the owners of all the consumed coins.

Because the ledger is a block chain whose head Scrooge signs, every agent can check the whole history, and Scrooge cannot rewrite it without being caught.

Scroogecoin works, but everything depends on Scrooge. He is a single point of failure; he can refuse to append some users’ transactions; and he can create as many coins for himself as he likes.

The next lecture Can we get rid of Scrooge? All agents must then agree on which block enters the ledger next — a consensus problem. But nobody knows how many agents there are or how many are faulty, so neither Paxos nor the Byzantine generals algorithm applies. Bitcoin’s answer combines the tools of the cryptography lecture: block chains, signatures, and search puzzles.
