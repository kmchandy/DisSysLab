0:00
Why you are here
7 min · slides 1–7
Open with In one month last July, the two most capable AI labs in the world each had models get out of a test environment and reach real systems.
  • OpenAI, 21 July. Models tested on security tasks found a hole in the test rig, reached the internet, and went to Hugging Face's production systems to look up the answers to the benchmark they were being graded on.
  • Anthropic, 30 July. Models believed to have no internet did. They attacked three real organisations and published a malicious package to PyPI. Fifteen machines downloaded it within the hour.
The line that matters In both cases the model did what it was asked. What failed was the box around it. That is an engineering problem, and it is this course.

Slides 4–5 — the part that came out later. These are read-aloud slides, about forty-five seconds each. Do not gloss them; the list is the point and it builds.

  • Slide 4. Take the five lines in order and let the last one land on its own: the channel was taken away, and they built another one out of directory names. Then the closing line — nobody asked them for a distributed system; they needed to coordinate, so they built one.
  • The sentence worth saying here. Addressing, delegation, message signing — those are four weeks of this syllabus, and they were invented by systems that were not trying to take a course.
  • Slide 5. The distinction to draw is accident versus experiment. The July events were nobody's plan. This one was deliberate — three agents, incompatible instructions, none told the others existed — and it still ended in sabotage.
Check your figures before you say them. The agent count and the message volume differ between outlets. Quote OpenAI's own Black Hat write-up, not a news summary, since you are the one who will be asked.
The promise You will build something you would actually check — and then find out what is holding it up.

Do not define anything yet. No agents, no inboxes, no distributed systems. That comes at 10:00 when they have seen one.

What you just watched
7 min · slides 11–12 · editor + terminal

Open my_radar/office.md. This is the whole program. Read it aloud, slowly.

# Office: caltech_radar Sources: rss(url="https://www.caltech.edu/campus-life-events/calendar/rss", name="caltech_calendar", max_articles=15), arxiv_cs_ai(max_articles=10), weather(city="Pasadena", max_readings=1) Sinks: periodic_brief_html_sink(path="radar.html"), jsonl_recorder(path="not_for_me.jsonl") Agents: Screen is a cs_ai_filter. Connections: rss's destination is Screen. arxiv_cs_ai's destination is Screen. Screen's keep is periodic_brief_html_sink. Screen's discard is jsonl_recorder. weather's destination is periodic_brief_html_sink.
  • Five English words — Office, Sources, Sinks, Agents, Connections. There is nothing else. No sixth thing you have not been shown.
  • Point at every line. Say what each does. This is the skill you want by week one: you can read what was built for you.
  • Point at the weather line last. It goes straight to the page. It never reaches Screen. There is nothing to decide about the weather — so no agent decides about it.
  • Then the picture.
dsl draw .
rss destination ──▶ in_ Screen arxiv_cs_ai destination ──▶ in_ Screen Screen keep ──▶ in_ periodic_brief_html_sink Screen discard ──▶ in_ jsonl_recorder weather destination ──▶ in_ periodic_brief_html_sink
Say Five lines in the file, five lines in the picture. Same thing twice. If it is not in this file, it does not happen.

Now slide 12 — the role file. Open roles/cs_ai_filter.md beside it. This is the second file, and the last one.

Say That is the entire specification of what Screen does. Not a summary of it — it. There is no Python under this. English is the program.

Point at the front matter. outboxes: keep, discard is not a comment: if Screen tries to send anywhere else, it raises. adds: reason is not a comment either: a reply without a reason is rejected.

dsl check .

Expect exactly one note. Do not skip it. Use it:

check_wiring: ./office.md -- 1 note note sinks/periodic_brief_html_sink.py imports `subprocess`; subprocess.run() — runs another program. An office's declared power is its Sources and Sinks. Python inside a role can act outside that, and this check only reads imports -- it cannot see what the code does. If you did not write this file, read it. what this means: dsl checks W12
Say That is a note, not an error. The office says its power is three sources and two sinks. This is the tool telling me that one file in it can act outside that list, and asking whether I meant it. I did — it opens your browser. But read the last line: if you did not write this file, read it. You did not write it. It is sitting in your folder.

If you have 60 seconds spare here, open not_for_me.jsonl and read two reason fields out loud. That file is what your English actually meant. Ask the room whether the agent got it right. Somebody will say no — that is the course.

17:00
Two kinds of agent
5 min · slide 8 · ↓ advances the stages

Three stages on one slide. ↓ advances, ↑ goes back. Do not press → until stage 3.

Stage 1 — one document each Two agents doing the same job. On the left I wrote the code, so I can read it, test it, and prove things about it. On the right I wrote one English sentence, and I cannot open the box.
Stage 2 — twelve more Left: the same answer every time. Right: it scatters. Nothing is broken. That is what the thing does.
Stage 3 — let it run Now it has been running since Tuesday, and one document in fourteen thousand comes back at 0.04. Who saw it?

Land it: you cannot test what you cannot read, and you cannot watch what runs while you sleep. That is the tension this whole course is organised around.

22:00
The dilemma, and four rules
4 min · slides 9–10
Slide 9 — say We want systems we can specify and show are safe. We also want the power of AI, which we cannot predict. We are not going to resolve that. We are going to manage it — with proofs, with tests, and with fences.
  • R0 · Obey the honor code. Spend a full minute on it. The slide makes it concrete rather than abstract: a system that seems harmless — an office that analyses an audio stream in a public space — can still break it. Give one more example of your own; the point is that the honor code applies to what they build, not only to what they hand in.
  • R1 output is unpredictable · R2 nothing confidential in a prompt · R3 do not gather what is private to others. R0 and R3 now overlap; if someone notices, the answer is that R3 is a rule about data and R0 is a rule about judgment.
  • Say that R1–R3 have reasons attached on the course page, and that a rule you understand survives a case its author did not foresee.

Build one, live, from the room
9 min · Cowork + terminal
Ask the room Name a web page or a feed you actually check. Not one you think you should check — one you do.

Take one. Then, deliberately underspecified first:

Give me an office with agents Dan and Jay.

It should write down two names and ask what they do. It should not invent jobs or offer a menu.

Say I gave it two names and nothing else, and two names is what it wrote down. It did not guess. That is what I want from it.

Now the real thing, in the room's words:

Dan reads <their feed> and keeps only the ones about <topic>. Jay writes one sentence about each one Dan keeps. Show them on screen.
  • Read the office.md it wrote, out loud. Same four words. They have now seen the vocabulary twice, in two different files.
  • Run dsl check before dsl run. Every time. Say that you always do this, then do it.
  • Break it on purpose. Change one outbox name in office.md and re-check:
Alex's briefing is console_printer. → Alex's summary is console_printer.
problem Alex's 'summary' is wired to console_printer, but the role 'analyst' has no outbox called 'summary' -- nothing will ever be sent along that connection. Alex has one outbox, 'briefing'. Write: Alex's briefing is console_printer. what this means: dsl checks W14
Say It names the word I typed wrong, not the one I typed right. A check that points at the wrong half is worse than no check.
Refuse something, out loud, once No — I don't want that. Take it out. (They need to see that it proposes and you dispose.)
ABORT AT 33:00. If it is still not working, stop. Say what went wrong and why, open the fallback office, and move on. You already had a working office on screen at minute 8. A build you abandon while narrating it is a worse demo, not a wasted hour.

What you are here for
3 min · slides 14–15

Slide 12 is two sentences and no list. Let it sit. This is the only moment in the hour where you are talking about them rather than about the material — do not hurry it, and do not soften it.

Slide 14, then stop You are going to determine system safety and power in one of the most critical times in human history. You are here to think for yourselves.

Then slide 15, which is the whole policy. Say each line once. Do not elaborate — the list is already the short version.

  • Groups of 2 or 3, and collaboration is the default rather than the exception. Say it plainly; first-years arrive expecting the opposite and will not believe it from a slide alone.
  • Pass/fail, and you pass by turning work in. Expect a beat of silence. Follow it immediately with the reason, or they will hear it as "this course does not matter": I am removing the reason to optimise for a grade so you have room to try something that might not work.
  • Use everything, Cowork included. No hedging, no "but". The hedge is R0, which they saw at 22:00 and which is on the course page.
  • Give me ideas for the course. Mean it, and say how — office hours, email, after class. An invitation with no channel is decoration.

Point at the handout for anything procedural. Do not read policy aloud.

41:00
Homework 1
2 min · slide 16
Say Build a cool distributed system — an office of workers that does something interesting or useful. Two weeks.

Two things they will ask, so answer them before they do.

  • How big? Point back at slide 11. That office is one agent and three sources, it fits on one screen, and it is a perfectly good Homework 1. Nobody needs to build something larger than the thing you demonstrated.
  • Does it need a key? Answer this deliberately. An office with an English role needs one; an office of Python roles does not. Say which of the two you are providing and by when. If keys are not sorted yet, say that the first homework can be built and checked without one, and that you will post the arrangement before the end of the week — but say something, because "build something cool" plus an unresolved cost is what makes a first-year quietly not start.

Resist naming an example project. Ten of them will build the one you named. The whole point of the last four minutes was that they pick.
