1. You don't know precisely what an LLM will say in response to a given input and prompt. That is both the strength and weakness of LLMs.
  2. Don't put anything confidential inside a conversation with an LLM. Don't enter passwords, financial information, or other data unless you are absolutely confident that the LLM you use will deal with the data appropriately.
  3. Do not use sensors that gather confidential information, and don't use sources of information that may be private to other people. An audio sensor may seem harmless but may accidentally gather private information.
  4. In this course, we don't allow an AI agent to create another AI agent. A danger is that the created agent may create more agents. Allowing agents to create agents is powerful; but we choose not to exercise that power.
  5. You can build systems in which AI agents share arbitrary information. For example, build a system with a shared whiteboard that agents read and write. Agents can create prompts and write them on the whiteboard. So, agents can completely change their behavior based on what they read. Creating new prompts and new agents allows for the collective behavior of a multi-agent system to evolve. In this course, we build multi-agent systems and we design them so that their behaviors satisfy specifications - we do not want their behaviors to evolve in unpredicatable ways. We constrain how AI agents communicate by limiting communication to flow along a static network that you define. The AI agents that ship with this framework have fixed prompts. They can change values of their parameters -- eg scores that determine whether an article is positive, neutral, or negative -- but they do not substantially change behavior
  6. We are careful about which devices outside our system are accessed -- external websites, sensors, actuators. A system that generates new external devices (eg new urls) for the system to access are powerful but potentially dangerous. We choose not to do this.
