Multi-agent systems

The safety of systems built from many agents. We prove algorithms correct with UNITY, a small fragment of temporal logic; we demonstrate programs correct by testing them systematically; we check what goes into and out of every agent; and we fence the whole thing in. Along the way you build a distributed system you would actually check.

Every page is HTML — press P to present Graded pass/fail

Lectures

Each lecture is one page. Read it before or after; it is the same page either way. What goes into the four labs, and the demos in order, is in labs.html.

  1. Introduction to distributed systems

    Two labs lost control of their agents in one month, and what came out later was that the agents had built a distributed system in order to coordinate. The dilemma this course lives in, the four rules, and a program that watches your own campus.

    Open

  2. Model of a distributed system

    Agents, inboxes, outboxes, connections, and the four axioms everything later is proved from. Two agents and one channel are enough to raise every question in the course.

    Open

  3. Lab: building distributed systems

    What the framework will do once you ask it for more than a pipeline: the kinds of agent it ships, offices built out of offices, and the coordinators — select, gate, match, synchronizer — that hold a queue still until an answer comes back.

    Open

  4. Message Ordering

    A computation drawn as a picture — a line for every agent and every one of its boxes, and a state as a vertical line. Then, with no clocks: what each agent can record, the dataflow graph built from those records, and which orderings of them are computations.

    Open

  5. Global Snapshots

    Photographing a system whose parts are all still moving. Why no instant is available, which collections of local snapshots are states the system could have been in, and the algorithm that finds one — proved from the previous class's theorem.

    Open

  6. Lab: watching and extending a system

    Start from an office that senses something real, then take it apart: trace and snapshot a run, kill it and resume from a checkpoint, read the Python the office compiles to, find out what dsl check refuses to run, and write a source or a sink of your own.

    Not written yet

  7. Logical time, termination and deadlock detection

    An office that cannot tell it has finished. Build the case, watch it hang, then read the algorithms that fix it.

    Not written yet

  8. Lab: AI skills

    Extend a contract that already has a checker — the backtester — then write the contract, and the checks, for a domain you care about. Then try to pass your own checks while being wrong. And what each agent's choice of model costs to run.

    Not written yet

  9. Lab: agents that create agents, fenced in

    Least privilege at the boundary, capabilities that can only be split, a budget that bounds how much a population can try, and a shared write-once whiteboard. Alongside it, the primary reports on agents that got out — the reports themselves, not the coverage of them.

    Not written yet
